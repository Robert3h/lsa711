The five layers

Each piece takes a real event from my own life and digs down through five layers (Event → Emotion → Pattern → System → Meta-cognition), ending with a question you can take with you and apply to yourself.

  1. L1EventWhat happened▾

    Facts only: who, when, where, what was said, what was done. Keep facts apart from your interpretation; note the interpretation down and save it for later.

    ASKIf you only had what a camera could record, what happened in that moment?

    E.G.At last Wednesday’s team meeting, my manager said in front of everyone, "Redo this report." (Not "my manager always shoots me down.")

  2. L2EmotionWhat you felt▾

    Just hold it; don’t rush to interpret. Notice where you feel it in your body, how strong it is (1–10), and whether more than one feeling is there at once.

    ASKIn that instant, what did you actually feel? Not what you should have felt, but what was really there.

    E.G.Embarrassed, wronged, and a little angry.

  3. L3PatternHas this reaction shown up elsewhere?▾

    Name other situations yourself and look for what they share. A pattern has to be spoken or confirmed by you, not announced by someone else.

    ASKDo these situations have something in common?

    E.G.Whenever my effort is dismissed in public, I feel an urge to prove myself right away.

  4. L4SystemWhat factory setting is running underneath▾

    A few angles help: what you want to protect, what you value, what you fear, what your usual strength turns into when overused. These are angles for asking, not a diagnosis.

    ASKIf there were a rule behind this reaction, how would it be written?

    E.G.I care a lot about being seen as reliable.

  5. L5Meta-cognitionWhat blind spots does my view have▾

    Step back and check whether your reading is just one version: am I understanding myself, or defending myself?

    ASKHow would a third person who was there describe the same event?

    E.G.Maybe my manager was just in a hurry, not dismissing me.
